This is a personal, non-commercial blog. There are no ads, no sponsors, no monetization, and nothing here is ever sold or shared with advertisers. This page explains the little data the site does collect, why, and what you can do about it.

What’s collected

Page views (analytics). This site uses GoatCounter, a privacy-friendly analytics tool that doesn’t use cookies and doesn’t build a profile of you. It records aggregate, anonymous counts — which pages were viewed, roughly where from, what browser — never anything tied to you personally.

Comments. If you post a comment, the name (or nickname) and text you enter are stored and displayed publicly on that page, along with the date. This is the only place on the site where you’re asked to type in anything personal, and it’s entirely optional — you only see it if you choose to comment.

Reactions (claps/likes) and read counts. Clicking a reaction button, or just reading a post, is recorded as an anonymous count tied to a random identifier stored in your browser — not to your name or any other personal detail. This exists purely so the “claps” and “reads” counters on each post work and don’t double-count your own repeat visits.

Fonts. Site fonts are hosted directly on this domain rather than loaded live from Google Fonts, so visiting this site doesn’t send your IP address to Google just to render text.

Where it’s stored

Comments, reactions, and read counts are stored in Firebase/Google Cloud Firestore, operated by Google. Depending on where you’re located, this may mean your comment data is processed outside your country. Google provides standard contractual safeguards for this as part of Firebase’s terms of service.

Your rights

If you’re in the EU/EEA/UK, you have rights under GDPR to access, correct, or delete your personal data, and to object to its processing. If you’re in Canada, PIPEDA gives you similar rights to access and correct your information. In practice, for this site, that mostly means: you can ask to have a comment removed.

To request removal, email asynkrunner@proton.me with a link to the page and roughly what the comment said or when you posted it, so it can be found — comments here aren’t tied to an account or verified email, so an exact description is the only practical way to confirm which comment is yours. Requests are handled within a few days, and always within 30 days.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top will be updated.
