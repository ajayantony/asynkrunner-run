Foolproof Rib Roast with Pan Gravy

🍳 Food American Easy
5 minPrep
4 hrCook
6Servings
📖 Adapted from Allrecipes

Our Thanksgiving centerpiece this year. The method sounds wrong the first time you read it: an hour in the oven, then three hours with the oven off and the door shut. But it’s almost entirely hands-off, and it works.

Ingredients

  • 1 (5 lb) bone-in standing rib roast
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 2 tsp ground black pepper
  • 2 tsp garlic powder

Instructions

  1. Take the roast out of the fridge an hour before you start so it comes up to room temperature.
  2. Heat the oven to 375°F and set a rack inside a roasting pan.
  3. Stir together the salt, pepper, and garlic powder.
  4. Set the roast on the rack, bones down and fat side up, and rub the seasoning all over it.
  5. Roast for 1 hour.
  6. The crucial step: switch the oven off and leave the roast inside for 3 hours. Don't open the door, not even once, because the trapped heat is what cooks it through.
  7. After the 3 hours, take the roast out, let it rest for 10 minutes, then carve and serve.

💡 Tip: If the roast needs more time, or you want to serve it hot later, turn the oven back on to 375°F about 40 minutes before dinner and roast for another 30-40 minutes, until the center reads at least 145°F on an instant-read thermometer. Pull it earlier if you like it medium-rare.

Pan Gravy

📖 Adapted from Your Homebased Mom

Made from the drippings left in the roasting pan, so start it while the roast rests. Makes about 2 cups.

Ingredients

  • All the drippings from the roasting pan
  • 4 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 2-3 cups beef broth, divided (1 cup for the roasting pan)
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Instructions

  1. Pour 1 cup of beef broth into the roasting pan and swish it around to loosen all the drippings and browned bits stuck to the bottom.
  2. Pour everything from the pan, broth and drippings, directly into a saucepan over medium heat.
  3. Whisk in the flour a spoonful at a time, whisking constantly so it doesn't go lumpy.
  4. Slowly whisk in more broth, about a cup at a time, until it's as thick or thin as you like.
  5. Keep stirring over medium heat until it thickens. Add more broth if it gets too thick.
  6. Taste before seasoning. The drippings already carry the roast's rub, so you may need very little salt and pepper.
